# Chemistry: student testing guide

A short, neutral session to see whether a chemistry student can find, understand and use the
Chemistry part of STEM Explorer. It is about the app, not about the student: there are no right or
wrong answers, and struggling is useful information.

**Nothing in the redesign has been validated with students yet.** Automated tests check that pages
load, links work and calculations are correct; they cannot tell whether an explanation is clear.
This session is how we find out.

## Before you start

- Use the live site: <https://stem-explorer-nine.vercel.app/>. Start every task from the Home page
  (open the link fresh, or press **← Home**).
- Do the session on a laptop first, then repeat tasks 1 and 5 on a phone.
- Ask the student to **think aloud**: say what they are looking for, what they expect, and what
  they notice.
- **Do not coach.** Don't point at the screen, don't name buttons or sections, and don't explain
  chemistry during a task. If the student is stuck for about two minutes, note where, then say
  “Let’s move on” and start the next task from Home.
- Note what they click, where they hesitate, any words they re-read or ask about, and anything they
  say. Timing is optional.

## The five tasks

Read each task out exactly as written.

1. **“Find an explanation of what molarity means.”**
2. **“Using the app, work out the concentration of a solution made by dissolving 0.40 mol of salt
   in water to make 2.0 L of solution.”**
   (Expected answer: 0.20 mol/L. Note whether they find and use the worked example or the
   calculator, and whether they keep mol, L and mol/L apart.)
3. **“Find out why a water molecule has the shape it has.”**
4. **“You have a 1.0 M stock solution and need 100 mL of 0.25 M solution. Find out how much of the
   stock solution you need.”**
   (Expected answer: 25 mL of stock, made up to 100 mL. Note whether they confuse the final volume
   with the amount of water added.)
5. **“Find the molecule with the formula C2H6O and tell me how many atoms it has. Then go back to
   where you searched.”**
   (Expected: ethanol, 9 atoms. Note whether the search query is still there when they return.)

## After each task, ask

1. Could you find it?
2. Which words were unclear?
3. What did you expect to happen?
4. Where did you get stuck?
5. Can you explain the idea in your own words now?

Write down the answers in the student's words; don't rephrase them.

## At the end

- “What was the most confusing part?”
- “What would you change first?”
- “Would you use this to study? For what?”

## Recording results

For each task, note: found it (yes / with difficulty / no), the path taken, unclear words, where
they got stuck, and their own-words explanation. Bring these notes back to the next development
step: changes should come from what the student actually did, not from what we expected.
